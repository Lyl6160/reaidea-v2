# Investigation Question

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

# The Problem

Engineering projects generate increasing amounts of information over time.

Examples include:

• Drawings

• CAD models

• Specifications

• Emails

• Meeting notes

• Test reports

• Calculations

• Customer feedback

• Change requests

• Risk registers

While these records preserve information, they rarely preserve engineering understanding.

As projects evolve, the reasoning behind important decisions gradually becomes fragmented or disappears.

This creates engineering discontinuity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

# Existing Practice

Current engineering systems generally excel at storing information.

Examples include:

CAD systems.

PLM systems.

Document management systems.

Project management systems.

Requirements systems.

Issue tracking systems.

These systems organise engineering artefacts.

They do not continuously maintain the current engineering understanding of the project.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

# Engineering Observation

Projects rarely fail because information is unavailable.

Projects often struggle because understanding becomes inconsistent.

Different people understand the project differently.

Assumptions become invisible.

Original reasoning becomes difficult to reconstruct.

Confidence becomes disconnected from evidence.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

# Proposed Solution

The Engineering Digital Twin continuously maintains the project's current engineering understanding.

Rather than preserving only information, it continuously represents:

Project Vision

Current Understanding

Evidence

Assumptions

Constraints

Opportunities

Risks

Engineering Decisions

Decision Rationale

Learning

Project Evolution

Every specialist engine contributes to maintaining this shared understanding.

Better decision consistency.

Transparent reasoning.

Improved project resilience.

Continuous project understanding.
