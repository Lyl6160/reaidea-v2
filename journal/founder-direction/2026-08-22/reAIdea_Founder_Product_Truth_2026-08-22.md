# reAIdea Founder Product Truth

Status: Founder direction lock  
Date: 22 August 2026  
Authority: This document defines the intended product experience. Implementation and construction records must conform to it; they must not reinterpret it.

## 1. Product promise

**Describe your invention once. REV brings it to life, investigates it, and develops it with you.**

The inventor supplies the idea. REV performs the difficult organising, reasoning, research, preparation and continuity work.

The locked principles are:

- Tell REV once.
- REV does more work.
- Ask only the smallest missing question.
- Do not force a questionnaire.
- Show useful output before asking for more effort.
- Every bench shares the same evolving Project understanding.
- The Workshop is alive and useful when the inventor enters.
- Assumptions, preliminary research and generated concepts must be labelled honestly.

## 2. What reAIdea is

reAIdea is a persistent AI engineering partner and Living Workshop for developing an invention from an early thought into an increasingly evidenced, visualised, testable and investable Project.

It is not:

- a long invention form;
- a collection of disconnected AI reports;
- a compulsory Engineering questionnaire;
- a system that makes the inventor manually populate REV's internal data model;
- a source of unqualified patent, legal, commercial or engineering certainty.

## 3. Golden inventor journey

### Stage 1 — Tell REV the idea

Home presents one clear invitation:

> Tell REV what you want to invent, in your own words.

The inventor may provide:

- one natural-language description;
- an optional photo, sketch or document;
- an optional invention name.

The primary action is:

> BRING MY IDEA TO LIFE

There is no visible engineering form and no requirement to know technical language.

### Stage 2 — Immediate first value

REV uses the first description to prepare:

- a concise reflection of what it understands;
- an early Concept 01 visual;
- explicit working assumptions;
- an understanding/readiness indication.

The concept may be labelled as an early non-authoritative working concept. It does not have to be perfect before it is useful.

### Stage 3 — Smallest clarification

REV asks only questions that materially improve or safely unblock the first useful outputs.

Rules:

- zero to three clarification questions is the normal path;
- one question is shown at a time;
- questions are generated from actual gaps, not a fixed checklist;
- REV infers reasonable details and labels them as assumptions;
- the inventor may choose **LET REV RECOMMEND** or **I'M NOT SURE**;
- additional clarification is optional unless one genuine blocker prevents a safe recognisable concept;
- no long interview is launched.

When ready, REV says:

> I have enough information to get started.

### Stage 4 — Enter the Living Workshop

Workshop entry must feel like arrival at an informed, active workplace—not the beginning of another form.

On entry:

- REV is visibly present;
- Concept 01 is on the centre stage or visibly being prepared;
- the Prototype bench pulses while representation work continues;
- other benches illuminate or pulse as their first contributions are prepared;
- the Inventor bench is optional, not a mandatory gate;
- each bench already knows the same Project description, REV understanding and accepted assumptions.

### Stage 5 — Output-first benches

Each bench opens with useful prepared content. Its default structure is:

1. What REV understands.
2. What REV prepared.
3. Sources, assumptions, confidence and limitations where relevant.
4. One recommended next decision or smallest missing clarification.

The question/input area is secondary. It appears when the inventor chooses to refine the work or when REV has one genuine blocker.

### Stage 6 — Refine the Project

The inventor reviews, corrects, chooses and refines through natural language. REV carries every accepted change across the whole Project.

Concept history must preserve revisions. Interactive 3D should be provided when the available information supports valid geometry. Where it does not, REV must show the best honest representation and explain the smallest missing detail needed.

## 4. Bench contracts

### Inventor

- Preserves the original idea and intent.
- Shows REV's current understanding and assumptions.
- Accepts optional corrections or missing thoughts.
- Never becomes a mandatory gateway to the rest of the Workshop.

### Engineering

- Infers the functional definition, components, operation, constraints and unknowns.
- Does not expose its internal checklist as a compulsory user questionnaire.
- Asks only one genuine blocker at a time.

### Prototype

- Presents Concept 01 and subsequent revisions.
- Supports 2D and interactive 3D when valid geometry exists.
- Preserves revision history and explains representation limitations.

### Testing

- Prepares an initial test strategy, measurements, likely failure points and safety considerations.
- Distinguishes recommendations from completed validation.

### Patent / IP

- Provides preliminary, sourced prior-art and similarity findings.
- Clearly states uncertainty and that the result is not legal advice or a patentability determination.

### Manufacturing

- Suggests likely materials, processes, assembly approach, suppliers or cost drivers.
- Labels estimates and assumptions honestly.

### Marketing

- Prepares likely customer groups, value proposition, differentiation, positioning and routes to market.
- Distinguishes sourced market evidence from AI inference.

### Reality

- Evaluates feasibility, practical limits, commercial risk, regulation, cost and next milestones.
- Brings conflicting bench findings together for a realistic decision.

## 5. Golden investor journey

The investor experience is an evidence-led view of the evolving Project, not the inventor's working questionnaire.

It presents:

1. The problem and intended user.
2. The proposed invention and visual concept.
3. Interactive model or best available representation.
4. Customer and market opportunity.
5. Competitors and differentiation.
6. Preliminary IP landscape.
7. Engineering and manufacturing feasibility.
8. Testing requirements and major risks.
9. Assumptions, evidence quality and unresolved questions.
10. Funding purpose and next development milestones.

The investor must be able to distinguish verified facts, sourced research, REV inference, founder statements and future work.

## 6. Competitive position

reAIdea must match the low-friction first-value experience of fast invention tools while exceeding them through:

- persistent Project continuity;
- connected specialist benches;
- evolving concept and revision history;
- interactive 3D where supportable;
- sourced research and visible confidence;
- an ongoing engineering partnership rather than a one-off report.

reAIdea does not compete by asking more questions. It competes by doing more useful work with fewer questions.

## 7. Non-negotiable experience tests

A build fails the product direction if any of the following occurs:

- the inventor must answer a long fixed sequence before receiving first value;
- Workshop opens as an empty shell waiting for manual bench completion;
- Engineering requires the inventor to populate REV's internal structure;
- separate benches repeat questions already answered elsewhere;
- a generated image, research claim or feasibility judgement is presented as proven truth;
- code or documentation changes the journey without founder approval;
- an implementation detail becomes the product authority.

## 8. Change-control contract

Before production code is changed, the proposal must state in plain language:

- the user problem;
- the exact inventor-journey moment affected;
- the exact investor value affected;
- behaviour before and after;
- a storyboard or mock-up for presentation changes;
- permitted files and protected systems;
- provider, storage and cost implications;
- browser acceptance steps;
- failure conditions;
- rollback checkpoint.

No production code is committed or pushed until the founder accepts the browser evidence.
