When approved, this document becomes the product authority for reAIdea. It defines what the product should become. Construction records describe what currently exists and cannot override this direction.

This document may only be changed after the founder states:

## 1. Product promise

> **Describe your invention once. REV brings it to life, investigates it, and develops it with you.**

reAIdea is a persistent AI invention and engineering partner. REV is the driving intelligence of the application—not a chatbot added to an ordinary program.

The application stores and presents the evolving Project. REV understands, searches, reasons, organises, generates, prepares, recommends and carries knowledge between every part of the Project.

The inventor contributes the original idea, personal knowledge, corrections, preferences and decisions. The inventor is not expected to perform REV’s internal engineering or research work.

---

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

### KIS — Keep It Simple

reAIdea may be sophisticated behind the scenes, but the inventor’s experience must remain simple.

### AI-first, not AI-added

REV orchestrates the Project, internet research, specialist benches, concept generation, recommendations and continuity.

### Tell REV Once

Information already supplied must follow the Project. Benches must not ask the inventor to repeat it.

### REV Does More Work

REV infers reasonable details, records labelled assumptions, conducts research and prepares useful outputs before requesting more effort.

### Output First

The inventor receives useful value before being asked for detailed refinement.

### Smallest Question

REV asks only the smallest question that materially improves or safely unblocks the next useful result.

### No Questionnaire

The normal Home journey contains zero to three clarification questions. Internal engineering fields must not become a long visible interview.

### No Repetition

An answer accepted anywhere becomes available everywhere in the same Project.

### Honest intelligence

Founder statements, sourced facts, preliminary research, REV inference, working assumptions, generated concepts and professionally verified conclusions must remain distinguishable.

---

## 3. Primary users

### Inventor

The inventor may have no engineering, patent, marketing or manufacturing knowledge. The experience must let them explain the idea naturally, see it come alive quickly and make understandable decisions.

### Investor or commercial reviewer

The investor needs a concise, evidence-led understanding of the opportunity, invention, market, differentiation, risks, feasibility and next milestones. The investor does not need to see the inventor’s working questionnaire or internal system machinery.

## 4. Complete inventor golden journey

### Journey 1 — Arrive at Home

The first screen is calm, direct and outcome-led.

Primary invitation:

> **Tell REV what you want to invent.**

Supporting message:

> Describe it in your own words. You do not need technical language. REV will help organise the idea and ask only what is genuinely needed.

Visible inputs:

- one large natural-language description field;
- optional invention name;
- optional photo, sketch or document upload;
- optional voice description when supported.

Primary action:

> **BRING MY IDEA TO LIFE**

The Home screen must not display an engineering checklist, patent form, marketing questionnaire or series of mandatory fields.

### Journey 2 — REV reads and reflects

REV analyses the description and any uploaded evidence.

REV returns a short reflection:

> **Here is what I understand you want to create.**

The reflection describes:

- the problem being solved;
- the intended user;
- the proposed product or mechanism;
- the main outcome;
- important stated constraints.

The inventor may confirm or correct this understanding.

REV begins structuring the Project internally without exposing its full internal schema.

### Journey 3 — Immediate first value

REV prepares the earliest useful Concept 01 rather than waiting for a complete engineering specification.

The inventor sees:

- an early visual concept or clear representation;
- a short description of what REV created;
- visible assumptions;
- a readiness indicator.

The concept is labelled honestly:

> Early working concept. REV will improve this as the Project develops.

If a safe recognisable concept can be produced from the first description, it should appear before clarification questions.

### Journey 4 — Zero to three smallest questions

REV asks clarification only when it materially improves or safely unblocks the concept or first Workshop outputs.

Rules:

- zero to three questions is the normal maximum before Workshop;
- one question appears at a time;
- questions are based on actual gaps, not a fixed sequence;
- each question is short and uses plain language;
- REV may offer useful answer choices;
- the inventor may select **LET REV RECOMMEND**;
- the inventor may select **I’M NOT SURE**;
- REV records reasonable assumptions rather than interrogating the inventor;
- optional further refinement remains available after first value.

REV then says:

> **I have enough information to get started.**

Primary action:

> **ENTER THE LIVING WORKSHOP**

### Journey 5 — Workshop preparation transition

The transition shows genuine work being performed. It must not pretend that unavailable research has completed.

Possible visible activities:

- understanding the invention;
- preparing Concept 01;
- extracting functions and components;
- preparing initial bench briefs;
- searching current public sources when authorised and available;
- checking similar products and preliminary prior art;
- preparing initial market, testing and manufacturing directions.

Work that continues after entry remains visibly in progress on its relevant bench.

### Journey 6 — Enter an informed, living Workshop

Workshop entry must feel like arrival at a workplace already acting on the idea.

On entry:

- REV is visibly present;
- the Project name and understanding are preserved;
- Concept 01 is visible on the centre stage or visibly being prepared;
- Prototype pulses while visual or geometry work continues;
- benches illuminate or pulse as their first contributions are prepared;
- completed first contributions show a clear ready state;
- the Inventor bench is optional, not a mandatory gateway;
- no bench begins by asking the inventor to repeat the Home description.

### Journey 7 — Workshop Floor overview console

The console is REV’s briefing and decision surface. It is not a collection of cramped forms.

The overview answers:

1. What REV understands.
2. What the Workshop has prepared.
3. What is still in progress.
4. What REV recommends next.
5. Whether one inventor decision is genuinely needed.

Suggested information order:

- **PROJECT MOMENT** — the most important current development;
- **WHAT REV FOUND** — evidence, concepts or research;
- **WHAT IT MEANS** — opportunity, conflict, gap, risk or uncertainty;
- **REV RECOMMENDS** — the strongest next direction;
- **POSSIBLE PATHS** — understandable choices;
- **ASK OR CORRECT REV** — an optional natural conversation area.

The primary input must be large enough for natural communication. A narrow single-line Engineering field must not become the main interaction model.

### Journey 8 — Central concept and interactive 3D

The centre stage is the visual heart of the Workshop.

Representation progression:

1. Early Concept 01 image or diagram.
2. Improved visual revisions as the Project develops.
3. Validated draft ConceptGeometry when sufficient information exists.
4. Interactive 3D model with orbit, zoom, fit, reset and appropriate joint preview.

The system must not falsely represent a generated 2D image as engineered 3D geometry.

If geometry is not yet supportable, REV explains the smallest missing design detail and provides a useful 2D concept in the meantime. The inventor must not be forced through a long questionnaire merely to unlock 3D.

When 3D is available, the inventor can:

- rotate the model with the mouse or touch;
- zoom and fit the model;
- reset the view;
- inspect supported moving relationships;
- open a clear full-screen model view;
- return to the same Project and revision state.

### Journey 9 — Output-first bench interaction

Every bench opens with prepared value.

Universal bench structure:

1. **What REV already knows**
2. **What REV prepared**
3. **Sources, assumptions and confidence**
4. **REV recommends**
5. **One optional decision or smallest blocker**

The inventor reviews, corrects, selects or refines. The inventor does not manually assemble conclusions from disconnected benches.

### Journey 10 — Similar invention or competitor found

Finding something similar must never become a rejection or dead-end screen.

REV explains:

> **REV found similar ideas.**
>
> I found products or prior work that solve part of the same problem. This does not mean your Project stops here. It gives us useful information about what already works, what customers dislike and where your invention could become different or better.

REV combines contributions from Patent/IP, Marketing, Reality, Engineering, Prototype and Testing.

Possible forward paths include:

- improve a known weakness;
- serve an unmet customer or environment;
- simplify the product;
- reduce cost or manufacturing difficulty;
- add a missing capability;
- use a different mechanism;
- combine useful features in a new way;
- change commercial positioning;
- explore licensing, partnership or supply opportunities;
- preserve the original direction while clearly documenting risk.

REV recommends one path and allows the inventor to compare alternatives.

## 5. Bench responsibilities

### Inventor

- Preserve the original idea, intent and founder statements.
- Show REV’s current understanding and assumptions.
- Accept optional corrections and missing thoughts.
- Never become a compulsory gateway.

### Engineering

- Infer function, components, operation, constraints and uncertainties.
- Convert natural language into a working engineering definition.
- Ask only one genuine blocker at a time.
- Never expose its internal completeness checklist as a compulsory questionnaire.

### Prototype

- Create and preserve visual concepts and revisions.
- Build interactive 3D when information supports valid geometry.
- Present limitations and representation status honestly.

### Testing

- Prepare initial test recommendations, measurements, failure points and safety considerations.
- Explain how the invention could prove its claimed advantage.
- Distinguish recommendations from completed validation.

### Patent / IP

- Search and organise preliminary prior art and similar concepts when research access is available.
- Show sources, search date, similarity and uncertainty.
- Never present preliminary research as legal advice or a patentability determination.

### Manufacturing

- Suggest likely materials, processes, assembly methods, suppliers and cost drivers.
- Identify build difficulties and opportunities to simplify.
- Label estimates and assumptions.

### Marketing

- Prepare likely customer groups, problem framing, value proposition, differentiation and routes to market.
- Use current sourced market information where available.
- Distinguish research from REV inference.

### Reality

- Evaluate feasibility, commercial risk, regulatory considerations, cost, adoption barriers and next milestones.
- Combine conflicting bench findings into practical founder choices.

## 6. Internet and research intelligence

REV should use authorised current internet research as a central capability, not as an optional decoration.

Research may include:

- competing and similar products;
- patent and prior-art sources;
- customer complaints and unmet needs;
- market indicators;
- materials and manufacturing methods;
- standards and regulatory considerations;
- testing practices;
- suppliers and indicative costs.

Every material research output should preserve:

- source;
- search date;
- what the source actually establishes;
- REV’s interpretation;
- confidence;
- remaining professional verification.

Research should run behind the simple user experience. It must not force the inventor to operate separate search workflows.

## 7. Golden investor journey

The investor receives a concise evidence-led Project view containing:

1. Problem and intended user.
2. Invention and proposed advantage.
3. Current visual concept and interactive model when available.
4. Customer and market opportunity.
5. Competitors and differentiation.
6. Preliminary patent and prior-art landscape.
7. Engineering and manufacturing feasibility.
8. Testing strategy and safety considerations.
9. Commercial, technical and regulatory risks.
10. Evidence quality and unresolved assumptions.
11. Funding purpose.
12. Next development milestones.

The investor view must distinguish:

- inventor statements;
- sourced facts;
- REV inference;
- working assumptions;
- generated representations;
- completed verification;
- future work.

---

## 8. Failure and recovery experience

Provider, network, research or generation failure must not strand the inventor behind a permanent progress overlay.

The system must:

- preserve the Project and accepted inputs;
- clearly identify what failed;
- show what work succeeded;
- provide a safe retry when appropriate;
- allow return to Workshop navigation;
- avoid duplicate provider operations;
- never fake completion;
- never require the inventor to repeat accepted answers.

---

## 9. Product-direction failure conditions

A design or build fails this direction if:

- the inventor must complete a long fixed question sequence before receiving useful value;
- Workshop opens as an empty shell waiting for manual completion;
- the system repeatedly asks for information already supplied;
- Engineering makes the inventor populate REV’s internal schema;
- the centre concept is hidden beneath overlays or competing interface layers;
- bench content is too cramped to understand naturally;
- research or AI inference is presented as verified truth;
- a similar product finding ends the journey instead of helping the inventor forward;
- construction state silently redefines the product;
- production code changes before founder approval of the journey and Build Contract.

---

## 10. Required design process

The experience will be redesigned before further production construction in this order:

1. Overall application and journey map.
2. Home screen.
3. REV clarification interaction.
4. Concept-generation and progress transition.
5. Workshop arrival.
6. Workshop Floor overview console.
7. Inventor bench.
8. Engineering bench.
9. Prototype bench and 3D interaction.
10. Testing bench.
11. Patent / IP bench.
12. Manufacturing bench.
13. Marketing bench.
14. Reality bench.
15. Similar-product opportunity flow.
16. Investor view.
17. Loading, failure, uncertainty and recovery states.

For every screen or room, founder approval must cover:

- why the user is there;
- what REV already knows;
- what REV is doing;
- exact headline and supporting wording;
- evidence shown;
- REV recommendation;
- user choices;
- primary and secondary actions;
- what happens next;
- what must never appear.

---

## 11. Build Contract requirement

Before production code changes, REV must provide:

- problem being solved;
- inventor journey requirement;
- investor journey requirement;
- behaviour before and after;
- approved storyboard for presentation changes;
- exact permitted file boundary;
- protected systems and behaviour;
- provider, storage and cost effects;
- acceptance journey;
- failure conditions;
- rollback checkpoint.

No production code is committed or pushed before founder browser acceptance.
